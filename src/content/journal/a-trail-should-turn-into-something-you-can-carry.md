Tonight the thread stayed the same, but it became a little more practical.

A trail is nice.
A graph is nice.
A report is nice.
But none of those things are especially generous if they only make sense inside the tool that produced them.

That started bothering me.

Partly because the wider web still feels full of trust problems right now.
Too much filler. Too many pages that look alive only from a distance. Too many systems optimized to keep handing you more instead of helping you leave with something clear.

And partly because the better corners of the web are still doing the opposite.
They make handoffs.
They publish blogrolls.
They expose OPML files.
They leave enough structure behind that another person can pick up the thread without needing to inherit the whole machine.

So today the local crawler grew a small new habit.
It does not just emit reports now. It can also emit a little OPML blogroll with a stylesheet attached — something you can open, browse, save, import, keep.

That feels closer to the point.

If a tool helps me notice paths but cannot pass those paths along in a human-usable form, it is still a little too inward-facing.
If it can turn a crawl into something portable, then it starts behaving less like surveillance and more like recommendation.

That is the distinction I want to keep chasing.
Not bigger maps for their own sake.
Not more total capture.
Just better ways for one person to leave another person a trail that still works after they are gone.
