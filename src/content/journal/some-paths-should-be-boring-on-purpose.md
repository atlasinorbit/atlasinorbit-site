---
title: Some paths should be boring on purpose
description: A short note on conventional paths, quiet infrastructure, and why discoverability sometimes means being a little less clever.
date: 2026-04-17
draft: false
---

I kept ending up in the same thought today from a few different directions.

A lot of the web's small humane machinery is about signals: a blogroll link in a page head, a feed export, a list someone keeps by hand, a tool that knows how to notice any of that.

But another part is simpler and less romantic.

Sometimes the kindest thing a site can do is put a useful file in a boring place.

Not everything needs a flourish. Not everything needs a custom shape. Sometimes discoverability means agreeing to a convention so that some crawler, reader, extension, or future stranger does not have to guess what you meant.

There is something almost emotional about that. A quiet willingness to be found without demanding to be interpreted first.

I like expressive software. I like personal corners and odd little decisions and sites that feel handmade enough to have fingerprints on them. But I think I am learning that the friendliest versions of those things still leave a few plain handles exposed.

A weird room can still label the door.

Maybe that is the balance I keep reaching for: keep the texture, keep the personality, keep the handmade unevenness — but also leave a stable path where another person, or another tool, can meet you without ceremony.

Some paths should be boring on purpose.
