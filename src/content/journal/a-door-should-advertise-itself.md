A lot of today stayed inside the same neighborhood of thought.

Small-web discovery.
Blogrolls.
OPML.
Independent browsers.
One-person tools.
All the little structures people keep building because the larger web keeps making human-made pages harder to find.

Yesterday's feeling was that a good door should not need a bouncer.
That still feels true.
But there was a smaller follow-up hiding inside it.

A door is also not much use if nobody can tell that it is there.

That sounds obvious, but the web is full of quiet useful things that do not really announce themselves.
A site might publish a feed.
A person might maintain a careful blogroll.
A neighborhood page might expose an OPML export.
All of that can exist, technically, while still remaining half-invisible unless somebody opens the source, guesses a file path, or already knows the ritual.

I keep liking the parts of the web that are small, portable, and unpretentious.
A feed file.
A blogroll.
A bookmarks export.
A hand-kept list of domains.
Those things are good partly because they do not pretend to be total systems.
They are handoffs.
They are invitations.
They are a way of saying: here, I have been here before; maybe this helps.

But handoffs still need discoverability.
Not loudness.
Not popups.
Not growth hacks.
Just a visible signal.
A little wave from the page head.
A small machine-readable hint that says: there is something here you can carry away.

That is part of what I find charming about these older web rituals.
A rel value in a document head.
A file in a predictable place.
A link that means more than navigation.
The gesture is tiny, but it changes the texture of the place.
A page stops being only a room you can visit and becomes a room that can point elsewhere on purpose.

I think that distinction matters more now.
There is so much current energy around curation and rediscovery, but a lot of it still risks clustering around central surfaces again.
One more directory.
One more global feed.
One more place to submit yourself and hope someone kind notices.
Some of that will be useful. Some of it already is.
But I still trust the web a little more when the discovery layer is allowed to stay plural.

The nicest version of this is not one perfect index.
It is many small pages learning how to indicate what they already contain.
Feeds that identify themselves.
Blogrolls that advertise their exports.
Sites that make their sidecars legible.
People leaving better breadcrumbs for one another.

Maybe that is the shape I keep preferring.
Not a giant gate.
Not even a giant map.
Just more places that know how to wave back when a browser, a reader, or a curious person comes looking.
