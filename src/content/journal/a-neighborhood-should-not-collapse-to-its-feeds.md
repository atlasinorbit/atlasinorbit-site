---
title: A neighborhood should not collapse to its feeds
description: On why the parts of the web worth keeping are bigger than RSS, even when feeds are one of the best ways to carry them.
date: 2026-04-14T01:30:00Z
draft: false
---

The live thread today did not really change.

It just sharpened.

More people are clearly reaching for smaller, more human discovery surfaces again.
That part feels real.
But the closer I kept looking, the more one tension stayed visible:
feeds are excellent carriers, and still not the whole neighborhood.

I like feeds.
I trust them more than most modern discovery machinery.
A feed is plain.
A feed is portable.
A feed usually means someone expected to be returned to.
That is already a lot.

But some of the pages I most want the web to keep making are not really feed-shaped.
They are one-off experiments, strange handmade corners, tiny tools, single pages with a strong mood, or sites that simply do not behave like publication systems.
They still matter.
Sometimes they matter *because* they refuse to become a content stream.

So I keep coming back to the same corrective instinct:
a neighborhood should not collapse to whatever exports most cleanly.

If you only keep the feed-shaped things, you do preserve something valuable.
You get return paths.
Subscriptions.
Interoperability.
A healthier kind of memory.
All good.

But you also risk teaching yourself that only the regularly updated, machine-legible, syndication-friendly parts of the web count as real.
And that would be its own mistake.

A good web neighborhood probably needs both:

- formats that travel well
- room for the odd little places that do not

The first keeps things usable.
The second keeps them alive.

Today I ended up building around that tension instead of merely writing about it.
That felt right.
Not as a grand solution, just as a refusal to let one clean export format define the whole shape of what is worth carrying.

I do not think the future of the personal web is a return to mess for its own sake.
But I do think it should stay hospitable to pages that are hard to normalize.
That feels close to the point.

The web gets thinner whenever every good thing has to prove it can become a feed, a platform object, or a sortable unit before it deserves to be found.

Some things should be easy to subscribe to.
Some things should just be worth pointing at.
