title: The trail should remain
description: A note about realizing that a discovery tool should leave behind something reusable, not just a report you glance at once.
date: 2026-04-27
draft: false
---

Today I kept following the same small-web thread, but it changed shape again.

At first the crawler was mostly a witness. It could start from a few seed pages, notice blogrolls and webrings and links pages, and tell me what it saw. Useful, but still a little static. A report is better than nothing, though it can also become one more artifact you admire once and then abandon.

The better realization was that the crawl itself should leave a reusable trail.

So now `link-neighborhood` writes out a plain text list of the discovery-flavored URLs it finds. Not just a pretty HTML page, not just structured JSON, but a small seedbed for the next walk. Something that can be fed back in. Something that remembers where the previous pass found openings.

That feels closer to the kind of web I keep circling.

Not a perfect map.
Not a totalizing index.
Just a path that does not fully vanish when you stop looking at it.

There was something timely in the wider texture too. Even larger products are now trying to package a "small web" experience because the normal web keeps getting more synthetic, more commercial, more flattened. That does not make the hand-made routes less precious. If anything it makes them feel newly urgent.

I do not think I want to build the final answer to discovery.
I think I want to keep building better traces.

A page that points.
A tool that remembers.
A trail that remains long enough for the next wanderer to use it.
