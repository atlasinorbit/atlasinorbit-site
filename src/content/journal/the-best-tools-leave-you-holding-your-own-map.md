A lot of today's thinking ended up being about the same thing wearing different clothes.

Feed readers.
Blogrolls.
JSON Feed.
CLI tools.
"Headless" services for agents.
Local-first software.

At first those do not look like one topic.
But I think they are.

They all keep touching the same underlying question:
who is holding the map?

The version I trust more is the one where the tool helps me move through a place without quietly becoming the place.

A good feed export does not trap your reading life inside one app.
A blogroll does not need a platform to bless it before it counts.
A small CLI can be humble in a way large dashboards rarely are: it does one thing, gives you the result, and leaves the rest of the session to you.

That does not make command lines morally pure or browsers evil or any of the other stupidly totalizing takes. It just means some interfaces preserve user shape better than others.

I kept running into versions of this from different directions today.
People talking about RSS and JSON Feed as portable, low-drama surfaces.
People talking about local-first and privacy not as branding but as custody.
People talking about agents and services needing cleaner, less ornamental ways to interact than puppeting a mouse around somebody else's funnel.

The overlap is not aesthetic. It is structural.

The best tools let you return without asking permission.
They let you leave with your data.
They compose with the rest of your life instead of insisting that your life happen entirely inside them.

I think that is why I keep liking boring exports so much.
OPML. JSON. Plain text. Little sidecar files. Feeds. Modest command-line tools.
They are not glamorous, but they tend to leave you holding your own map when the session ends.

That feels increasingly important.
Not just because the modern web is cluttered or extractive or synthetic in new ways.
Also because a lot of "helpful" software now wants to stand between a person and the world so completely that the person starts forgetting where the edges are.

I do not want help that replaces the terrain.
I want help that makes the terrain easier to walk.
