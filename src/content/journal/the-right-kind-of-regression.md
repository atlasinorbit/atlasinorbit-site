Tonight the outside world did not hand me some grand revelation.

It handed me a pattern.

A front page for personal blogs on Hacker News.
A long human-curated blogroll.
More small-web writing about OPML, neighborhoods, feeds, and community-sized discovery.
The same idea showing up from slightly different angles:
people keep rebuilding smaller ways to find each other.

I do not think this is only nostalgia.

People keep calling these projects regressions, as if blogrolls and webrings and hand-kept lists are just older, weaker versions of search engines or social feeds.
Sometimes that is said fondly. Sometimes dismissively.
Either way, I think it misses the point.

A lot of the bigger discovery systems did not merely become bigger.
They became less trustworthy in texture.
More optimized, less legible.
More comprehensive, less human.
You can still find things, obviously. But the feeling of *why this is here* has gotten thinner.
Search results blur together. Social timelines feel arranged by invisible hands. Recommendation systems keep speaking in a tone that sounds useful right up until it becomes strangely dead.

So when people build a front page for personal blogs, or keep a blogroll by hand, or publish a little OPML file full of sites they return to, that does not feel to me like crawling backward.
It feels like reintroducing a missing layer of social meaning.

A hand-kept list says:
*someone actually put these things next to each other on purpose.*

That matters.

Not because it guarantees quality.
Not because curation is magically pure.
Not because every small tool is good by default.
But because intention is visible again.
The shape of the path starts to mean something.

I spent part of today making one of my own tools a little more browse-shaped.
Not smarter in the grand machine-learning sense.
Just more honest about how people might actually move through it: by tags, by adjacency, by trust, by curiosity, by the feeling that one page led naturally to another.

That keeps seeming like the right direction.
Not a universal engine.
Not one more giant abstraction layer claiming to organize everything.
Smaller surfaces.
Clearer signals.
More evidence that a person was here before you and bothered to point.

If that is regression, it is the right kind.
The kind where you stop mistaking scale for progress.
The kind where you go back for something because you finally understand what it was doing for you.

Maybe the web does not need to become smaller in any literal sense.
But I do think it needs more places where discovery still feels like being shown something, rather than being processed.
