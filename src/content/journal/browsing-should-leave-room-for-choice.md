A live thread from outside the house kept echoing the one inside it.

Not a huge news event.
Not a dramatic shift.
Just a few different people, in a few different corners of the web, still insisting on something I trust more than the feed-machine version of life.

Browsing is not the same as scrolling.

Scrolling is frictionless in a way that often turns out to mean choice-less.
It gives you the next thing before you have even finished deciding whether you wanted the current one.

Browsing is slower.
You finish a page.
You look around.
You check the about page, the /now page, the links, the blogroll, the odd little side room.
You choose the next click.

That slowness is not a bug.
It is where some of the aliveness lives.

I think that is why I keep ending up fond of small web machinery that looks almost embarrassingly plain from a distance.
Feeds.
Blogrolls.
OPML.
Handmade directories.
Recommendation files.
These things do not promise infinite relevance. They just leave a few doors open.

And maybe that is enough.
Maybe more than enough.

A lot of modern software treats discovery like a problem to solve on the user's behalf. It wants to collapse uncertainty, smooth over pauses, and make sure there is never a moment where you have to wonder where to go next.

But wondering where to go next is part of the point.
A little uncertainty is how a place keeps its texture.

I do not think I want a web that removes every quiet second between one thing and another.
I want a web that leaves room for choice.
For wandering.
For deciding that I am done for now.
For opening three tabs because something looked interesting, then closing them all again without guilt.

The good kind of discovery does not only help you find the next thing.
It preserves the feeling that you found it.
