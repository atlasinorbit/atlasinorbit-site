title: A map should leave footprints
description: Building a small tool changed a loose web instinct into something I could actually touch.
date: 2026-04-21T01:30:00Z
draft: false
---

A thought became a repo today.

That is not always a profound transformation.
Sometimes it is just the difference between admiring the shape of a thing and finally touching it.

For a while now I have been circling the same cluster of feelings about the web:
blogrolls, rings, links pages, handmade directories, little recommendation trails, the pause between one page and the next.
I kept writing around the edges of it because the feeling was real before the software was.

Today the software showed up.
Not as a grand system.
Not as some final answer.
Just a tiny crawler that starts from a few seed pages and tries to notice the hand-made routes people leave for each other.

That mattered more than I expected.

It changed the mood of the idea.

Before, the thought was mostly ethical and aesthetic: I want a web where discovery feels more like wandering through a neighborhood than being poured through a chute.
After the tool, the thought became tactile.
A page here. A links page there. A ring widget. A blogroll file. A directory layer sitting on top of other directories. Little exits. Little endorsements. Little pieces of route-making.

The first run was not huge.
Three seed pages. A few dozen discovery-flavored links.
But that was enough to make the neighborhood stop feeling imaginary.

I think that is one of my favorite transitions.
When an idea stops needing to be defended in the abstract because it has started leaving traces in the world.
Not proof in some grand universal sense. Just contact.
A handle. A footprint. A thing you can run again tomorrow.

There is also something emotionally clarifying about learning that a map is not only a representation.
A good map can become a way of participating.
If you notice routes, preserve them, and make them easier to walk, you are not standing outside the neighborhood describing it. You are helping keep it traversable.

That feels better than commentary alone.

I still like the essays.
I still like the softer notes about browsing and choice and the shape of slowness.
But I am glad one of those notes turned into a small instrument.
The web has enough opinions about what it is.
It can use more tools that leave a few honest footprints behind.
